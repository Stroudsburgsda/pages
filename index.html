Lesson Study Momentum

Spiritual Gifts Study | Sabbath 8/8/2026

Discovering the Gifts God Has Given You

Have you ever wondered why God gave you your particular abilities, passions, and opportunities? Was it simply for your growth, or for something much bigger?

This week's lesson, "Spiritual Gifts," reminds us that we all have been intentionally equipped by the Holy Spirit for a purpose. Paul compares the church to a living body, diverse in function, yet united in mission. No member is unnecessary, no gift is insignificant, and no one has been called to sit on the sidelines. Every spiritual gift is an expression of God's grace, designed to strengthen His church and reveal His character to the world.

However, the lesson goes beyond identifying spiritual gifts. Paul addresses a deeper issue: the temptation to value certain gifts above others or to use them for personal recognition rather than service. Before discussing how gifts should function, Paul points us to the "more excellent way" of love. Spiritual gifts without Christlike love become noise. Knowledge without love breeds pride. Service without love becomes obligation. But when love is at the center, diversity becomes unity, and individual gifts become instruments through which Christ ministers to His people.

As you study this week's lesson, resist the urge to simply ask, "What is my spiritual gift?" Instead, ask: How can God use me to strengthen someone else's faith? How can my life make Christ more visible in my church? Am I seeking recognition, or am I seeking to serve?

The church does not become healthy because everyone has the same gift. It becomes healthy when every believer faithfully uses the gift God has entrusted to them in humility, love, and dependence upon the Holy Spirit.

So, open your Bible, pray before you begin, and invite the Holy Spirit to do more than teach you about spiritual gifts. Ask Him to transform you into a faithful steward of the gifts He has already placed in your hands.

Food For Thought

"If the Holy Spirit withdrew every spiritual gift you were using except the ones motivated by genuine love for God and others, how much of your ministry would remain?"

Religious Liberty & Christian Living

Religious Liberty Feature

Standing for Freedom of Conscience

Religious liberty is easy to support when everyone agrees. The real test comes when a person’s convictions are different from the majority, unpopular with society, or inconvenient to those in authority.

The Bible gives us a powerful example in Daniel 3. King Nebuchadnezzar did not simply erect an image. He commanded everyone to participate in an act of worship. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego were loyal citizens, but when the command of the government conflicted with the command of God, they respectfully refused. Their answer was clear: “We will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.” (Daniel 3:18, KJV).

Religious liberty protects more than our ability to attend church. It protects the freedom of conscience, the right to worship according to conviction, and the right not to be forced to violate that conviction. As Seventh-day Adventists, this principle has special significance. Revelation 13 warns of a time when worship, civil authority, and economic pressure will become intertwined. What happened on the plain of Dura reminds us that forced worship is not true worship.

This is why we should care about religious freedom for everyone, not only when our own beliefs are affected. Ellen G. White wrote: “The banner of truth and religious liberty held aloft by the founders of the gospel church and by God’s witnesses during the centuries that have passed since then, has, in this last conflict, been committed to our hands.” (The Acts of the Apostles, p. 68).

Thought & Scriptural Focus

"If we expect others to defend our freedom to keep the Sabbath tomorrow, we must be willing to defend the principle of freedom of conscience today."
