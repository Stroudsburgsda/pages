Midweek Momentum

1 Corinthians Study | Sabbath 8/1/2026

Does This Glorify God?

Have you reviewed this week’s Sabbath School lesson? In my work with students, they frequently ask, "Is this allowed?" As Christians, we sometimes find ourselves asking the same question. This week, Paul encourages us to ask instead, "Does this glorify God?" based on 1 Corinthians 10:31. The lesson emphasizes that every decision, whether big or small, can become an act of worship.

As Paul addresses the issue of idolatry, he broadens our understanding of what an idol really is. We know idolatry is not limited to statues or ancient temples; idolatry is anything that competes for our highest loyalty and affection. Knowledge of what we should and should not do is not enough. Christian maturity is measured by love—a love for God that shapes our choices and a love for others that willingly lays aside personal rights for their spiritual good.

This week's study also offers a timely reminder that spiritual privilege does not guarantee spiritual safety. Israel experienced God's miracles firsthand, yet many drifted because they became overconfident. Paul's warning is just as relevant today: we are to remain humble, flee from anything that pulls our hearts away from Christ, and trust that God always provides a way of escape when temptation comes.

Ultimately, the lesson calls us to live with intentionality. Christian freedom is never merely about exercising our rights; it is about using our freedom to build others up, point people to Jesus, and glorify God in every aspect of life.

Food For Thought

"Growing in Christ isn't simply about accumulating biblical knowledge; it's about allowing that knowledge to transform the way we think, serve, and love others. How is your spiritual maturity strengthening the unity of Christ's body rather than contributing to its divisions?"

Religious Liberty & Christian Living

Religious Liberty Feature

Called to Be Different

As God’s remnant people, we have never been called to blend in with the world. From the beginning, the Lord has called His people to reflect His character in every area of life, including the way we dress and adorn ourselves. The Seventh-day Adventist Church has consistently upheld the biblical principles of modesty, simplicity, and Christian stewardship, encouraging believers to avoid the wearing of ornamental jewelry and outward display that draws attention to self rather than to Christ. Our standards are not about earning salvation, but about revealing a heart that has been surrendered to Jesus.

In recent years, however, we have seen a gradual shift. Practices that were once clearly recognized as inconsistent with biblical counsel are becoming increasingly common, even among church leaders. When adults compromise, our children and youth naturally conclude that these practices are acceptable. Whether we realize it or not, every leader, parent, and church member is setting an example. The question is not simply, "Is it wrong?" but rather, "Does my life point others to Christ or to the spirit of the world?"

The Bible records a powerful example in Exodus 33. After Israel worshiped the golden calf, God called His people to repentance. One of the outward evidences of their renewed commitment was that they removed their ornaments before the Lord (Exodus 33:5-6). God was teaching them that true revival requires more than words. It requires a willingness to lay aside anything that competes with complete devotion to Him. Before they could move forward as His covenant people, they first had to separate themselves from the symbols of their past unfaithfulness.

As we near Christ’s return, Revelation describes a faithful people who "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (Revelation 14:12). In these final days, the enemy does not always attack through open rebellion. More often, he introduces gradual compromise until the distinction between the church and the world becomes difficult to see. May each of us, especially those entrusted with leadership, prayerfully ask the Lord to search our hearts. Let us lovingly uphold the standards God has given, not as a means of salvation, but as a joyful expression of our love for the Savior and our desire to be ready for His soon return.

Scriptural Focus
